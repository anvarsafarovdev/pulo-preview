    Revision 8, with your notes of 11 November on revision 7.
    • Tapping back: any step you have already filled can be tapped open again, in any order, even while dimmed. A step still empty stays shut until Next brings you to it. Next keeps its own order: it goes to the first thing still missing.
    • The last choice wins. Pick a due date, then open Repeats: the repeat replaces the one-time reminder, and the panel says so (“Replaces the one-time reminder on 14 Nov. Tap Repeats again to bring it back.”). Tap Repeats again and the due date you picked comes back. Installments behaves the same. What is open when you tap Save is what gets saved; a hidden due date is never sent with a repeat or a plan.
    • The plan's length reads in years and months: 23 payments is “1 year 11 months”, 24 is “2 years”. (Revision 7 showed a decimal, so 1.9 meant 1.9 years, about 1 year 11 months, not 1 year 9 months. The dates were right.)
    Revision 7, with your notes and answers of 11 November.
    • One step at a time. Only the step being filled is lit; the rest is dimmed and cannot be touched. Next dims the finished step and lights the next. Tap a dimmed earlier step to reopen it; Next then takes you on to whatever is still missing.
    • The amount shows just the 0, with no blinking cursor.
    • “When is it due?” The keyboard closes when Next lands here, so the whole screen is free for the wheel and the two cards.
    • Repeats and Installments open in the middle of the screen, clear of the line above Next. On a plan without the option, a small lock pulses inside the panel (“Starter”, “Pro”) while they try it.
    • Next is Telegram’s own bottom button (your choice): Telegram draws it outside the page and keeps it above the keyboard on every phone and on desktop. Our colour and words; Telegram’s shape and font. The short line above it is Pulo’s. The grey keyboard here is drawn only to show where things sit.
    • A Repeat is a reminder on this one debt, on the days the shop picks (every Wednesday, every 5th), until it is paid. Each reminder shows what is still owed, so a part payment lowers the next one. Photo and Note are back for it: it is an ordinary debt. The wheel: Every month / Every week, then the day or the weekday. A day after the 28th falls on the last day of shorter months.
    • Installments: the summary (“12 payments of … / Every 11th of the month, until …”) sits above the slider, so your finger never hides it.
    • The person page: a running repeat shows “Reminds every Wednesday · Until paid · next Wed 18 Nov”, with a Stop (your answer). When the plan lapses: paused, and it resumes on the same days when the shop pays again; dates missed meanwhile are not sent (your answer). A repeat is set only in Record a debt for now (your answer).
    Gone: the repeat that recorded a new debt every cycle (the “rent” repeat) and its words “Pulo writes …”, “until you stop it”, “Repeat started”. Words are provisional English.
